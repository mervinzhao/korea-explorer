Where Korea sits on the value chain

Korea leads the world in end products and is strong in base-metal refining, but it is thin in the battery and rare-earth intermediates that connect the two and almost absent from mining at home.

Mining
Negligible at home
In KoreaKorea's net import reliance for critical minerals exceeds 99.7%. Sangdong tungsten, back in production in 2026 after closing in 1994, is the notable exception.
Korean-owned abroadSelective. POSCO owns lithium brine in Argentina, KOMIR holds a large minority stake in Madagascar's Ambatovy nickel-cobalt mine, and cell and cathode makers hold equity-plus-offtake deals in Australian lithium. KOMIR's overseas role is now being restored after a decade of divestment that ended with the Boleo mine sold for $2 in 2025.
Smelting and refining
Strong in base metals
In KoreaKorea Zinc's Onsan complex turns out more than a million tonnes a year of over ten metals, including about 630 kt of zinc, the largest output of any single smelter. Young Poong, the world's sixth-largest zinc producer, runs the Seokpo smelter, and LS MnM runs Korea's only copper smelter at roughly 600 to 680 kt/yr of cathode. Battery chemicals are emerging through POSCO's lithium hydroxide and nickel refining. Rare-earth separation, magnesium, titanium sponge and polysilicon are absent.
Korean-owned abroadKorea Zinc's Sun Metals zinc refinery in Australia and a Tennessee smelter planned from 2029, POSCO's 25 kt/yr lithium hydroxide plant in Argentina, LS Eco Energy's rare-earth metal plant in Vietnam due from late 2026, and Korean equity in Indonesian nickel intermediates.
By-product minor metals
A hidden strength
In KoreaKorea Zinc recovers antimony, indium, bismuth, tellurium, cadmium, silver and gold from its circuits, with germanium due in 2027 and gallium targeted for 2028. Onsan produces about 10 t of antimony a day.
Korean-owned abroadThe Tennessee smelter is designed for 12 metals, 11 of them on the US critical list.
Chemicals and intermediates
Thin
In KoreaThe weakest link for batteries and magnets. China supplied 96.6% of cathode precursor imports in 2023, spherical graphite comes almost entirely from China, and 88% of NdFeB magnets were Chinese in 2022. LG Chem and others are building precursor plants. High-purity semiconductor chemicals have been a strength since the 2019 localization.
Korean-owned abroadLS Eco Energy's Vietnam plant would supply rare-earth metals for magnets it plans to make in the US.
Materials and components
Strong
In KoreaCathode materials (POSCO Future M, EcoPro, LG Chem, L&F), silicon wafers (SK siltron, fifth in the world) and battery copper foil. POSCO Future M is Korea's only natural graphite anode maker.
Korean-owned abroadSeveral materials makers have built plants near Korean cell factories in North America and Europe.
End products
World-leading
In KoreaMemory chips (over 60% of the global market), OLED displays, LNG carriers, cars and steel. Semiconductor exports alone reached $173.4 billion in 2025.
Korean-owned abroadKorean battery makers produce about a fifth of the world's cells and run more than 400 GWh of capacity outside Korea, far more than Japanese or Chinese makers abroad.

What the data show

Korea's exposure sits in processed forms more than in ore

The deepest single-country dependencies are on refined and intermediate products: cathode precursors (96.6% from China in 2023), lithium hydroxide (about 80%), NdFeB magnets (88% in 2022) and indium (94.2% in 2025). Korea's stated goal is to bring dependence on any single country down to 50% by 2030.

Export controls redirect trade quickly where alternative capacity exists

After China's 2023 controls, its share of Korea's gallium imports fell to 26% and its share of germanium to 15% by 2025, as Germany, the US, Japan and Canada filled the gap. Indium, graphite and tungsten moved the other way over the decade, because no comparable capacity was ready.

Import statistics cannot tell diversification from rerouting

Antimony imports shifted from China to Thailand in 2025, but China itself imports Thai and Myanmar concentrate. Whether Korea's exposure actually fell depends on where the metal was mined and smelted, which customs data does not record. Buyers will soon need that proof: from January 2027, US defense procurement bars Chinese content in magnets, tungsten and tantalum from the mine onward.

Supply shocks have come from several directions

Fluorine chemicals in 2019, urea in 2021, rare earths from 2025 and helium in 2026 each ran through a single processing point or shipping route. The 2019 response shows how fast Korea can move: domestic suppliers qualified high-purity hydrogen fluoride within months.

Dates that matter

  • 1 January 2027US defense procurement rules extend the ban on Chinese, Russian, Iranian and North Korean content in rare-earth magnets, tungsten and tantalum to every stage from mining onward, at every subcontract tier.
  • 10 January 2027China's suspension of its October 2025 rare-earth controls now runs to this date, after a two-month extension MOFCOM confirmed on 28 September 2026. Its April 2025 licensing of seven medium and heavy rare earths stays in force.
  • 18 February 2027EU battery passports become mandatory for EV, light-transport and larger industrial batteries.
  • 18 August 2027EU battery due-diligence obligations apply, including supply-chain checks for cobalt, lithium, nickel and natural graphite.

About this draft

  • Sourcing-risk tiers draw on 2025 KOMIS import data as analyzed in The Diplomat (June 2026), KITA and Korea Customs figures for 2022 and 2023, IEA and GlobalData concentration data, and live export-control measures. Shares from different years and product definitions are not directly comparable. KOMIS puts China at 71% of 2025 graphite imports, for example, while KITA put synthetic graphite at 93.7% in 2023.
  • Opportunity tiers and value-chain levels are analytical judgments, a starting point for discussion rather than a scored index.
  • The element set follows the 33 critical and 10 strategic minerals designated in February 2023, plus four unlisted elements where Korean industry is exposed: praseodymium, germanium, helium and fluorine. Energy, refined petroleum, petrochemicals and emissions are out of scope.
  • Bill-of-materials entries are informed estimates rather than teardown data. Company capacities and timelines are company-reported.
  • Live situations to refresh: the January 2027 deadline on China's suspended rare-earth controls, helium supply after the 2026 Ras Laffan outage, copper treatment charges and KOMIR's restored overseas role.
